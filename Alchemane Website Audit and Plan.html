Executive summary

The new build is a large step up from the Shopify site in structure, copy and photography consistency. It is not launchable yet. The purchase journey is broken, the numbers the site uses to earn trust contradict each other, and several sections still hold placeholder or template content. Fixing those is a two-week job. With the decisions taken on 15 Sep (checkout plus consultation, hero-only scroll film, micro-interactions elsewhere), the revised plan is 36 working days for Astra, about seven and a half weeks, with the home page signed off on day 12.

Five things that block launch. Cart and checkout return 404 while every product shows Add to cart and Buy it now. Search and account icons are disabled. Product prices differ by page. Testimonials are the same five quotes relabelled per product page. Every product shows "Only 5 items left in stock", including a made-to-order ₹80,000 wig.

What works. The information architecture is right for this business: concern-led entry points, one long-form page per solution, a consultation form on every page, comparison tables, FAQ per method and a single consistent product shoot. The Figma system is coherent and the build follows it closely on desktop.

Where it falls short of premium. The visual language is a competent generic e-commerce template: teal buttons, rounded cards, catalogue-style product shots against grey, clickbait blog thumbnails, stock photos in the consultation and studio sections. A ₹40,000 purchase that involves someone's hair loss needs quieter, more assured design, real people with names, and product photography that shows construction close up.

What works

  • Clean, light header; wordmark, four links, city pill, three icons. Mobile drawer in Figma is clear with grouped accordions.
  • Figma mega menu mirrors the old Shopify taxonomy (Clip-In, Permanent, Fringes, Toppers, Volumizer, Wigs) so customers who know the old site find their way.

Issues

  • Search and Account are dead controls. Buttons carry disabled and aria-label "Search — coming soon" / "Account — coming soon" on every page. A disabled icon in a premium header reads as unfinished.
  • Shop menu is not the Figma mega menu. Server HTML for Home lists only the four topper products plus View All under Shop; Figma shows six columns with 21 items. Needs confirming in a browser (could be client-rendered), otherwise 17 products are unreachable from the menu.
  • "Book a Consultation" dropped from primary nav. Old site had it in the header; it is the site's main conversion and now lives only in page bodies and a floating widget.
  • City selector purpose unclear. Live shows "Select city"; Figma shows "Bangalore" selected. The only studio link in HTML is /studios/mumbai. Behaviour after selection not verifiable; if it routes to /studios/* pages it implies studios that do not exist (see studios).
  • Cart icon has no count and no destination. Cart is a button with aria-label "Cart"; /cart returns 404.
  • Header appears in 3 of 116 empty-alt images? No: header icons have alt text via aria-label, fine. Logo is an SVG with alt "Alchemane Home". Good.

Recommendations

  • Either ship search (product and article search with a simple client index) or remove the icon until it works. Same for account.
  • Restore "Book a Consultation" as a visible header CTA, styled as the one filled button in the header.
  • Implement the six-column mega menu from Figma with images for the five solution groups; on mobile keep the accordion drawer.
  • Decide the city selector's job: if it only pre-fills the consultation form, move it into the form. If it switches studio content, it needs real studios.

Needed

Decision on search scope; decision on city selector; confirmation of the Shop taxonomy (six groups or three solutions).

What works

  • Split layout, single phone field, WhatsApp alternative and consent line are all sensible for an Indian audience. Dark green panel is the most premium-looking element on the site.

Issues

  • Build deviates from Figma. Figma: "Luxury Hair Extensions" tag, three benefit rows with icons (Early Access, Premium Quality, Personalised Guidance), dark "Unlock My Offer" button. Live: different benefit copy, teal button, smaller card, no tag.
  • Fires immediately on first paint (it covered content in every screenshot at 0 s). Interrupting before the hero has been seen is the single most common reason luxury visitors bounce. No Figma spec for trigger timing, frequency or dismissal memory.
  • Mobile popup covers the whole viewport with the dark panel first; offer field is below the fold on a 390 px screen.
  • 5% is a weak hook on a ₹28,000 purchase; "free colour-match consultation" or "free guide" converts better for this category and avoids discount signalling.

Recommendations

  • Match Figma or update Figma; agree one version.
  • Trigger on 40% scroll or exit intent, not on load; suppress for 30 days after dismiss; never show on policy, consultation or checkout pages.
  • Mobile: single-column, offer field visible without scrolling.

Needed

Offer decision (5% off, free guide, or free consultation); SMS/WhatsApp provider and consent wording.

  • Route concern cards to solutions, not /shop; restore per-card CTA labels from Figma mobile.
  • Replace templated testimonials with five real, consented client stories, each with product, city and a real before/after pair. Until then remove the section.
  • Celebrity section: use the Lara Dutta film and any consented celebrity footage, or cut the section.
  • Founder: shoot a proper studio portrait; keep the first-person Figma copy.
  • Serve tiles through next/image as WebP/AVIF at 600 px; preload the hero poster; convert Coolvetica to WOFF2 or replace it (see direction).
  • Alt text for every content image; button contrast to AA; real labels on form fields.

Needed

Hero headline sign-off; five consented client stories with photos; celebrity footage rights; founder portrait; confirmation of studio address and name; press article URLs.

6. 3D, photography and video shortlist

Hero: AI-generated scroll film from your existing footage (Higgsfield)

Your idea, restated: take the current video of the model wearing extensions, generate a 360° turn, then a confident hair wave, then the hair growing longer and longer as the reader scrolls, with the figure moving right to left and left to right down to the end of the page. Two-thirds of that is a strong hero. The last third fights the page. Here is the version I would build.

Beat sheet (pinned hero, about two viewports of scroll)

  • Beat 1, Reveal (0–30% scroll). Model front-on, then a slow 180° turn to show the extensions from the back, then back to three-quarter. Headline "Hair that is unmistakably yours" and promise line sit left; the film fills the right.
  • Beat 2, Confidence (30–55%). The hair wave: one slow toss, hair settles. Caption "Colour-matched. Hand-finished. Fitted in our Khar West studio."
  • Beat 3, Length (55–100%). Hair lengthens from shoulder to waist as you scroll; a brass ruler on the left counts 14 → 18 → 22 → 26 in alongside, so the effect reads as product information (lengths you sell) rather than a trick. Two CTAs appear at the end: Book a consultation, Shop extensions.
  • Idle. At rest the film loops a 3-second slice of Beat 2 so the hero never freezes. "Watch the film" opens the full clip with sound.

"Throughout the page" without hijacking the page

  • A model that follows the reader across every section would cover product cards, forms and tables, cannot work on a phone, and forces every section to be designed around a moving figure. It would also push the scroll length of the home page far beyond what a buyer will tolerate.
  • Keep the spirit instead: a single fine strand (an SVG path in verdigris at low opacity) starts where the hero ends and lengthens down the page as you scroll, swapping from the right margin to the left margin at each section boundary, and finishes at the consultation form where it ends in a small brass clip. On phones it is hidden. It costs one developer day and nothing in performance. If you still want the figure to travel, we test it after launch on the Extensions page only.

Generating it in Higgsfield

  • Generate three separate 5–6 second clips, one per beat, all from the same source frame of your existing video so the face, top and lighting stay consistent. Locked camera, plain background, no hands near the hair (hands and clips are where AI video breaks). Ask for 25 fps, 16:9 and 9:16 versions.
  • Generate 4–6 candidates per beat and pick by three checks: hair strands do not flicker or merge, the face does not drift between clips, and the extension blend looks like your real product (Natural Black, wave pattern). Reject anything with extra fingers, jewellery changes or shifting necklines.
